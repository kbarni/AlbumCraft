AlbumCraft 0.9.0 is built on the Craft suite's shared libraries, reads HEIC and AVIF photos, shows colours right, and places pictures from GPS tracks.

**Under the hood: the craft-libs**
Large parts of AlbumCraft were rewritten to use [craft-libs](https://github.com/storytold/craft-libs), the libraries shared by the *Craft* apps. Bugs fixed there are fixed in every Craft app.
- **Metadata** (`craft-metadata`, `craft-tiff`): EXIF, IPTC and XMP are read and written by one shared library, which replaces three separate ones and AlbumCraft's own XMP code.
- **Saving** (`craft-atomic`): every file AlbumCraft writes is crash-safe: the library, the caches, XMP sidecars, thumbnails, collages, exported copies and imports. A crash or a power cut can no longer leave a half-written file. Exports and imports never replace a file that appears under the same name in the meantime.
- **File pickers** (`craft-dialogs`): open and save dialogs no longer block the window. On macOS this fixes a crash when a picker was opened.
- **Quitting on macOS** (`craft-quit`): closing the window no longer crashes AppKit.
- **Printing** (`craft-print`, `craft-ipp`): printing now works in the Flatpak. On Linux and macOS, AlbumCraft talks to CUPS directly when the CUPS programs are missing, and still uses them where they are installed so driverless network printers keep working. Paper sizes are shown as *A4*, *Letter* or *4 x 6 in*. On Windows, the printer's own settings dialog is used, and every platform sends the same PDF.
- **Colours** (`craft-cms`) and **HEIC / AVIF** (`craft-heif`, `craft-image-codecs`): see below.

**New**
- **Colour management**: pictures are shown in their true colours from their ICC profile (JPEG, PNG, TIFF, WebP, raw previews) or their HEIC / AVIF colour description. Display P3 photos from phones and Adobe RGB photos no longer look washed out. Thumbnails, edits, exports, smart search and prints all agree. Exported JPEGs carry the sRGB profile. Thumbnails are made again once after the update.
- **HEIC and AVIF photos** from iPhones, Macs and recent cameras, read by pure-Rust decoders. They are turned the way the file says, with their date, camera information, GPS and XMP notes. Notes are written to a sidecar file.
- **Locations from a GPS track**: *Locations from a GPS track…* in the picture menu and the folder menu reads one or more GPX files and gives each picture the position the track had when it was taken. Choose the pictures' time zone (the camera's, this computer's or a fixed one) and correct a camera clock that was slow or fast. The dialog counts how many pictures will be placed and suggests the time zone that places the most. Undo puts things back; the files are not changed.
- **Date taken**: *Date taken…* in the picture menu, or a click on the date in the info panel. Set one date for several pictures, move every date by the same amount (for a camera clock that was off), or go back to the file's date. A year or a month alone is enough for scanned prints. Sorting, folder dates and contact sheets follow, and exported copies carry the date in their EXIF and XMP. The original files are never changed.
- **Folders dated by their pictures**: a folder's header shows when its pictures were taken (one date, or the first to the last). Folders are ordered and listed by year by their first picture's date. Before, copying or editing one file could move a folder to the top.
- **Better XMP**:
  - Lightroom's keyword levels are no longer read as separate tags (*Sport* next to *Sport|Diving*). Tags read that way before are removed; tags added in AlbumCraft stay.
  - Without a sidecar file, the picture's IPTC caption and keywords fill what its XMP doesn't say.
  - Dates are read from and written to XMP.
  - Panasonic RW2 files now show their camera information.
- **For agents**: new `geotag_from_gpx` and `set_date_taken` commands.

**Downloads**
- Windows x64: `albumcraft-0.9.0-windows-x64.msi` (installer) or `albumcraft-0.9.0-windows-x64-portable.zip`.
- Linux x86_64: `albumcraft-0.9.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage`, `.deb`, `.flatpak` or `.tar.gz` (Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12 or newer).
- macOS (Apple Silicon): `albumcraft-0.9.0-macos-arm64.dmg`.

Everything the app needs comes with it. Video decoding uses FFmpeg, which is included. Only making a slideshow movie needs the FFmpeg program installed.

These builds are not signed. On macOS, right-click the app and choose *Open* the first time. On Windows, choose *More info* → *Run anyway* in the SmartScreen prompt.

**Known limitations**
- Colours are shown as sRGB: the monitor's own profile and printer profiles are not used yet. Adobe RGB pictures marked only in their EXIF, without a profile, are shown as sRGB.
- A date set in AlbumCraft is not written into the original file. Programs that read only the file, not the sidecar, still show the camera's date.
- XMP inside a file is changed only in JPEG and PNG files. For other files (HEIC and AVIF included), and for JPEG files with extended XMP (over 64 KB), the notes go into a sidecar file.
- Face regions written by other programs are kept as they are: a person removed in AlbumCraft but still named in a face region comes back at the next scan.
- Albums and folder notes are not written to XMP.
- Export, e-mail, web gallery, print, collage and movie use a video's thumbnail frame as a still picture; no trimmed copy of a video is made yet.
- Videos are decoded without the graphics card: 4K HEVC may not play smoothly on a slow computer.
- Live Photos and motion photos are not paired with their picture.
- Smart search and tag proposals look only at a video's thumbnail frame.
- Auto straighten can mistake one long roof or wall line seen at an angle for a horizon, and leaning natural shapes (trees, columns) for uprights. Undo or the Straighten slider puts it right.
- Face recognition is not there yet.
- Moved pictures are analysed again for smart search.
- *Open with* lists no programs on macOS.
- A `.tar.gz` copy updated by the built-in update keeps its old `libonnxruntime.so`.
- The MCP tools, the system's own error messages and EXIF values stay in English. Right-to-left languages are not supported.
- Raw files are not developed from sensor data; edits work on the embedded preview.
